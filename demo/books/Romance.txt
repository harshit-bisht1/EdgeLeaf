Chapter 1: The Wrong Umbrella

It rained the day Nora Hale took the wrong umbrella from the café on Bell Street, and it was still raining a week later when she finally carried it back, wrapped in brown paper and an apology she had rehearsed on the bus. The umbrella was dark green with a carved wooden handle in the shape of a heron, and it was, quite obviously, loved.

The man behind the counter looked at the parcel, then at her, then at the parcel again. "You brought it back," he said, in the tone of someone who had stopped expecting things to come back.

"I'm so sorry. Mine is black, with a broken spoke. I didn't look." She pushed it across the counter. "It must belong to someone."

"My mother," he said. He unwrapped it slowly and ran his thumb along the heron's neck. Then he smiled, and the café, which had seemed perfectly ordinary a moment before, became the sort of place a person might come back to every morning for no good reason at all.

Chapter 2: Every Morning at Eight

She came back the next morning at eight, telling herself it was for the coffee, which was in fact very good. She came back the morning after that because it would have been strange to stop. By the second week he had begun to set a cup on the counter when he saw her crossing the street, and she had begun to pretend she did not notice that he did.

His name was Samuel. He had taken over the café when his mother fell ill, meaning to stay for a season, and that had been four years ago. He read poetry between customers and was embarrassed when she caught him at it.

"It's for the shop," he said, holding up the book. "I write a line on the board each day. People expect it now."

That afternoon, walking past in a thin drizzle, Nora glanced at the chalkboard by the door. Beneath the price of scones, in careful capitals, someone had written: SOME THINGS COME BACK. She stood under her broken umbrella reading it for a long time, and did not mind at all that she was getting wet.
