Chapter 1: The Clock That Ran Backwards

Inspector Ada Mercer had seen a great many strange things in thirty years with the county police, but she had never before been called to a house because its clocks were running backwards. All eleven of them, the housekeeper said, wringing her hands in the doorway of Fenwick Hall. Every one had turned against the hour sometime between supper and breakfast.

"And Mr Fenwick?" Mercer asked.

"Gone, ma'am. His bed not slept in, his boots by the door, and the study locked from the inside." The housekeeper lowered her voice, as if the house might be listening. "He wound those clocks himself. Every Sunday, for forty years. He'd never let a soul touch them."

The study door yielded to a constable's shoulder. Inside, the fire had burned to ash, a glass of port stood untouched on the desk, and the great longcase clock in the corner ticked slowly, deliberately, toward yesterday. Mercer stood very still and listened to it. Then she took out her notebook and wrote a single word: rehearsal.

Chapter 2: What the Gardener Saw

The gardener was the only person at Fenwick Hall who did not seem frightened, and Mercer liked him at once for it. He met her among the frozen roses with his hands in his pockets and told her, without being asked, that he had seen a light in the study at three in the morning, moving from clock to clock like someone saying goodnight.

"One person?" she asked.

"One light," he said carefully. "I couldn't swear to the person. But whoever it was knew the house. Never once bumped a chair."

Mercer walked back to the hall along the gravel, counting her steps, turning the thing over. A man who wound his clocks every Sunday for forty years would know exactly how long it took to set them all running the wrong way. He would know, too, that anyone looking into his disappearance would look first at the time. She stopped on the doorstep, smiled, and went to find out what Mr Fenwick had wanted them to stop looking at.
