Chapter 1: The Map in the Lining

Juniper Vale found the map stitched inside the lining of her grandfather's old flying jacket three days after his funeral, and by the end of the week she had sold her bicycle, left her job at the post office and bought a seat on a cargo plane bound for the edge of the Atacama. Her mother called it grief. Juniper called it the first sensible thing she had done in years.

The map was hand-drawn on a square of silk, faded to the colour of weak tea. It showed a coastline, a range of mountains with no names, and a single red cross beside a symbol she did not recognise: a circle with a line through it, like an eye half closed.

Along the bottom edge, in her grandfather's tight, slanting hand, he had written: For June, when she's ready. Not before. She had been nine years old the last time he called her June.

The pilot of the cargo plane, a weathered woman named Rosa who spoke very little, looked at the silk map for a long time without touching it. Then she folded her arms. "I know that mark," she said. "My father knew your grandfather. They went up there together in 1971. Only one of them came back."

Chapter 2: Salt and Stars

They left the airstrip before dawn in Rosa's battered truck, heading east into a desert so dry that nothing rotted and nothing grew, where the ground was white with salt and the sky at night held more stars than Juniper had known existed. Rosa drove without headlights, steering by the shape of the mountains against the paling sky.

On the second day they found the first marker: a cairn of black stones with the half-closed eye scratched into the top one. On the third day they found the second, and beside it the rusted frame of a motorcycle, half buried, its handlebars pointing east like a compass needle.

"He came this way," Rosa said. It was not clear whether she meant Juniper's grandfather or her own father, and Juniper did not ask.

That night, lying in the bed of the truck under the blazing sky, Juniper unfolded the silk map and held it up against the stars. The mountains on the silk lined up with the mountains on the horizon. And the red cross, she saw with a jolt that brought her upright, did not mark a place on the ground at all. It marked a star.
