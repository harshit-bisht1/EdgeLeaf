Chapter 1: Signal at Dawn

The message arrived at 04:12 station time, threaded through the static between two dead satellites, and for eleven seconds nobody aboard the Halcyon understood that it was addressed to them by name. Engineer Tomas Reyes heard it first. He was alone on the observation deck with a cup of reconstituted coffee, watching the sun come up over a planet that had no business having a sunrise.

"Halcyon," said the voice. It was calm, patient, unmistakably human. "Halcyon, please respond. This is Halcyon."

Reyes put his coffee down very carefully. Their ship was the only vessel within six light-years. He knew every voice aboard her, and this one he knew best of all, because it was his own.

By the time the captain reached the deck, the signal had begun repeating with a slight delay, as though whoever was sending it were listening to the echo and correcting it. Reyes played it back three times. On the third, beneath his own voice, faint as frost on glass, he heard the ship's alarm. It had not sounded yet.

Chapter 2: The Other Halcyon

They found the second ship forty hours later, tumbling slowly in the shadow of the planet's largest moon. She was identical to the Halcyon down to the scorch mark on her port airlock, the one Reyes had made himself during a botched repair in their second year out. Her lights were dark. Her hull was cold. Her registration, when they queried it, matched their own.

Captain Imani Osei was the first to speak. "Nobody goes aboard," she said. "Nobody touches her. We log it, we leave, and we think about it from very far away."

It was a good order, a sensible one, and Reyes agreed with every word of it. He was still agreeing with it an hour later, suited and tethered, as he drifted across the gap between the two ships with the other Halcyon's airlock growing larger in his visor.

The lock cycled open before he touched it. Inside, the lights came up slowly, the way they did on his own ship when someone had been away a long while, and on the wall beside the inner door, in handwriting he knew as well as his own, someone had written: DON'T LET THEM ANSWER.
